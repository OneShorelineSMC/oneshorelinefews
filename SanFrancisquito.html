For the review

  1. Statuses. 50% of channel capacity = Stay alert, 70% = Be ready, road level = Flooding likely: assumed, to confirm with SFCJPA (and Palo Alto).
  2. Top of the page. The creek's status is the most serious of the three Palo Alto gauges, with each location below it, so a resident sees which reach. A gauge that hasn't reported in 45 minutes shows as not reporting and doesn't count; if all three are quiet, the status says so and points to Stanford.
  3. Rising fast. A Palo Alto gauge up 1 ft or more in the last hour is flagged "rising fast" (assumed, to tune with a season of storms).
  4. Datum. One datum for every gauge and its levels (assumed). Ask SFCJPA why the lower gauges read 1.5–2.5 ft in dry weather last January and 0–0.7 ft now.
  5. Thresholds. SFCJPA: Pope-Chaucer 13 / 16.5 ft. City of Palo Alto: Chaucer St 13.2 / 17.9 ft. Pick one.
  6. Cameras. The City of Palo Alto's camera snapshots at Pope-Chaucer and West Bayshore show here for the review; ask the City before going public.
  7. Stanford's levels. FEWS uses OneRain's levels for the Stanford gauge: 11, 12 and 12.8 ft. January's storm (1,070 cfs, the biggest this year) reached 4.83 ft, and today it reads under 0.5 ft. Confirm with SFCJPA or OneRain whether those levels are in the same datum as the USGS reading; if not, San Francisquito can't show above Normal on the Creeks page either.
  8. Travel time. "Water at Stanford usually reaches Palo Alto within 1 to 2 hours" comes from the Jan 3–7, 2026 storm; the Storm Tracker would refine it each season.
