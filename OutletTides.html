Shoreline low points (BCDC Adapting to Rising Tides)

The low points from the Shoreline page: where BCDC’s maps show the Bay coming over the shoreline at 12, 24 or 36 inches above an average high tide (MHHW). “Comes over at” is MHHW there (from the NOAA gauges, weighted by distance) plus that level, in ft NAVD88. “Margin” is the model’s highest water in the next 48 hours minus that level (positive = the Bay reaches it).

Datum check at the NOAA gauges

The model’s water level is relative to its own datum (about mean sea level). At each gauge, the offset to NAVD88 is the median difference between NOAA’s projection at the gauge (ft NAVD88) and the model at the gauge’s nearest model point. “Left over” is the spread that remains after the offset; a few hundredths of a foot means the method reproduces NOAA’s own projection there. Each outlet uses the gauges’ offsets weighted by distance.

How this works
  • Outlets: lookups/creek_outlets.csv once reviewed, otherwise the draft lookups/proposed/creek_outlets.csv (rows with include = no left out). Outlets at the same point are read once.
  • Shoreline low points: lookups/shoreline_hotspots.csv once reviewed, otherwise the draft lookups/proposed/shoreline_hotspots.csv (the Shoreline page’s list). The level where the Bay comes over is BCDC’s (MHHW + 12, 24 or 36 in), with MHHW from NOAA’s datums at the three gauges weighted by distance. The Shoreline page uses Redwood City’s MHHW everywhere, so its levels differ a little in the north.
  • Model: SFBOFS (FVCOM, about 100,000 points), from NOAA’s public archive on Amazon. Four runs a day (03, 09, 15, 21 UTC), usually available about 2 hours later; only the water level at the chosen points is read.
  • Model point: the nearest point at least 0.5 m below the model’s datum, so it stays wet at low tide. The nearest point of any depth is listed too; a big gap means the outlet is up a slough or behind marsh.
  • Comparison lines: at the nearest of San Francisco (9414290), Alameda (9414750) and Redwood City (9414523): the model at the gauge, NOAA’s projection there (should overlap the model line) and the tide table.
  • Log: each run’s peak at each outlet and the datum check go to outlet_tides_log.csv on the data branch (60 days), to compare with what happens.
  • Not included: creek flow, waves and wind setup at the shoreline; the model’s resolution in narrow channels.
