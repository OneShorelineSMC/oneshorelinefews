# November 2026

November brought one large storm, two smaller ones, and the highest tides of the season. On Nov 25 the Bay at Redwood City came within 2 inches of the highest water on record there, and the ocean at Monterey set a new record (preliminary until NOAA reviews it). High water flooded streets in South San Francisco and closed state highway ramps in Belmont and Menlo Park, with rain in Belmont. On the coast, large waves broke the end off the Pacifica Pier.

## Storms of Nov 13 and Nov 15 to 16

On Nov 13, nearly 2 inches fell at San Bruno's First Avenue gauge while a high tide ran 1.6 feet above average. San Bruno Creek reached Flood Watch and Colma Creek reached Flood Monitoring. The next day SR-1 in Pacifica closed for flooding between Crespi Drive and Linda Mar Boulevard. A smaller storm on Nov 15 and 16 put 1 to 1.3 inches on the coast.

## The storm of Nov 18 to 21

This was the wettest storm of the season so far. La Honda measured 4.9 inches and Pilarcitos Dam 4.5. San Bruno's gauge at First Avenue measured 4.4 inches. A storm that size has about a 1-in-16 (6%) chance in any year.

At 10:35 AM on Nov 18, heavy rain set Belmont Creek's status to "Flooding likely." The creek gauge was out for repair, so the rain trigger made that call on its own. About an hour later, SMC Alert announced that Industrial Road in San Carlos was closed for flooding between Harbor Boulevard and Quarry Road, in the Harbor Industrial area that Belmont Creek's warnings cover. It reopened at 2:46 PM. Just before noon, Atherton Channel reached its Flood Warning level, the point at which flooding is likely, and San Bruno and Colma Creeks reached Flood Watch.

Caltrans closed the southbound US-101 on-ramp at Marine and Harbor in Belmont from the afternoon of Nov 18 to late morning Nov 19, during heavy rain and a high tide. Belmont's gauge came back that evening, and early on Nov 19 the creek reached Flood Watch.

## King tides, Nov 22 to 25

The Bay rose higher each morning. At Redwood City it peaked at 11:48 AM on Nov 25, 2.5 feet above the average high tide and about a foot above the tide table. Only three days since gauge records began there in 1974 have been higher: Jan 3, 2026, Dec 3, 1983, and Dec 14, 2024. San Francisco had its highest November tide in 109 years of record, and Alameda its highest in 48. On the coast, Monterey beat its January 1983 record by about 2 inches.

South San Francisco reported tidal flooding on Nov 25 at five spots east of 101, including Harbor Way at Mitchell Avenue, Forbes Boulevard at DNA Way and two crossings of South Airport Road, and warned they could flood again with the next high tide. Caltrans closed four ramps on SR-84 at the Wildlife Refuge exit in Menlo Park on the morning of Nov 24; they were still closed on Nov 25. The National Weather Service issued Coastal Flood Advisories on 18 of the first 25 days and a Coastal Flood Warning for Nov 25 and 26.

On the coast, the same high water came with large surf (a National Weather Service High Surf Warning called for breakers of 20 to 30 feet). On Nov 25 the waves broke the L-shaped end off the Pacifica Municipal Pier, which had been closed since June 4 for cracks, and beachfront homes in Pacifica flooded. Pacifica closed the beach and the water near the pier, and on Nov 26 crews filled voids in the Beach Boulevard seawall.

## Looking ahead

Our [Tides page](https://oneshorelinesmc.github.io/oneshorelinefews/Tides.html) expects the Bay at Redwood City to stay at or near the flood level through Nov 28, with the Nov 26 high tide about 2 feet above the average high tide. The next runs it flags are Dec 4 to 8 and Dec 20 to 26. The Dec 20 to 26 run could rival this week's, about 2.5 feet above the average high tide, if the Bay keeps running as high as it has. Those December numbers are estimates (the tide table plus how high the Bay has been running), not forecasts, and a storm on those dates would add to them.
