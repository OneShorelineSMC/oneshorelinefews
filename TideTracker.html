Month by month

Days at or above a level count a day once however long the water stayed there; the hours show how long. In small type: the usual number of days for that month (average of the last 19 water years). “Above the tide table”: the month’s average of measured minus tide table, the non-tidal residual: the Bay running high or low for weeks (El Niño, warm water, coastal ocean waves) plus any storms and runoff. “Skew surge”: at each high tide, the highest measured water minus the tide table’s high for that tide, whenever the peak came; the month’s largest is shown. It measures how much higher a high tide came in than predicted, and unlike a minute-by-minute difference it isn’t thrown off by a tide that arrives a little early or late.

How this works
  • This season: NOAA’s 6-minute measured water level and tide table at each gauge, from Oct 1. For each day: the highest water and when, the tide table’s highest, measured minus tide table (the non-tidal residual: the day’s average and largest), the skew surge at each high tide, and the minutes at or above each level. Updated hourly; the last two days are recomputed as NOAA fills gaps. Today is partial.
  • Levels: MHHW (NOAA’s average higher high water at the gauge); NOAA’s minor high tide flooding level, derived the way NOAA does nationally (MHHW + 0.5 m + 4% of the tide range; about 9.0 ft at Redwood City); and staff levels from lookups/tide_tracker_levels.csv (Redwood City’s 1-year tide 8.3 ft and flood level 8.5 ft; Monterey’s 1-year tide 6.87 ft, provisional).
  • History: NOAA’s high and low water record at each gauge (each day’s highest high water), as far back as NOAA gives it in NAVD88. Rankings use every year with at least 80% of the month (or the season so far); “usual” counts and the shaded range on the graph use the last 19 complete water years. Older years were lower partly because the Bay has risen about 0.6 ft a century.
  • Historic days come from high and low waters, the season from 6-minute data, so a past day’s highest can be a little lower than a 6-minute peak would have been.
