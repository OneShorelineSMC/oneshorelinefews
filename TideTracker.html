How this works
  • This season: NOAA’s 6-minute measured water level and tide table at each gauge, from Oct 1. For each day: the highest water and when, the tide table’s highest, measured minus tide table (the day’s average and largest), and the minutes at or above each level. Updated hourly; the last two days are recomputed as NOAA fills gaps. Today is partial.
  • Levels: MHHW (NOAA’s average higher high water at the gauge); NOAA’s minor high tide flooding level, derived the way NOAA does nationally (MHHW + 0.5 m + 4% of the tide range; about 9.0 ft at Redwood City); and staff levels from lookups/tide_tracker_levels.csv (Redwood City’s 1-year tide 8.3 ft and flood level 8.5 ft; Monterey’s 1-year tide 6.87 ft, provisional).
  • History: NOAA’s high and low water record at each gauge (each day’s highest high water), as far back as NOAA gives it in NAVD88. Rankings use every year with at least 80% of the month (or the season so far); “usual” counts and the shaded range on the graph use the last 19 complete water years. Older years were lower partly because the Bay has risen about 0.6 ft a century.
  • Historic days come from high and low waters, the season from 6-minute data, so a past day’s highest can be a little lower than a 6-minute peak would have been.
