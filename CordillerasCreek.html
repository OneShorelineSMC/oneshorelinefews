Preview for staff review. Not linked from the public pages. To confirm before it goes public: Redwood City's number for reporting a blocked creek or a flooded street, and Redwood City's sandbag pickup.

Cordilleras Creek runs about 4 miles from the hills above Redwood City and San Carlos down to the Bay, much of it through backyards. It has no water-level gauge, so this page can't tell you how high the creek is right now.

Instead it compares the rain with New Year's Eve 2022, when the creek flooded homes in Redwood City's Centennial and Edgewood Park neighborhoods and on the alphabet streets.

What you can do

  • Get alerts. Sign up for SMC Alert, San Mateo County's emergency alerts.
  • If the creek runs past your home, that stretch is yours to keep clear: Redwood City and San Carlos both leave it to the property owner. Before the rain, take out branches, trash and anything that could wash down and jam a culvert or bridge. Stay out of the creek once it's running high.
  • Clear the storm drain on your street of leaves when it's safe to.
  • Sandbags. San Carlos: Corporation Yard, 1000 Bransten Road, weekdays 8 AM–3 PM, up to 10 bags. Unincorporated areas (such as Emerald Hills): San Mateo County's Grant Corporation Yard, 752 Chestnut Street, Redwood City; reserve pre-made bags first, (650) 363-4103.
  • Report flooding or a blocked creek. San Carlos: (650) 802-4140. If anyone is in danger, call 911.
  • Never walk or drive through moving water. Six inches of fast water can knock you down.

New Year's Eve 2022

4.6 inof rain in one day, Dec 31. One of the wettest days on record in Redwood City.
0.9 inin the heaviest hour, 1 to 2 PM. An hour that wet comes about once in 25 years.
4.1 inin 12 hours, 4 AM to 4 PM. More than the 12-hour rain expected once in 100 years.
SoakedThe ground was already wet: more than 2 in had fallen in the days before.
  • It came in two bursts, early morning (4 to 8 AM) and early afternoon (noon to 3 PM). The afternoon burst was the heaviest and came at low tide, so the flooding came from the rain, not the Bay.
  • Where it flooded: the Centennial and Edgewood Park neighborhoods and the alphabet streets, including G Street. Water was nearly 10 feet deep in the creek behind some homes, and at least four homes were badly damaged.

Sources: the Belmont rain gauge (OneShoreline / OneRain) and NOAA; reporting by Redwood City Pulse, Redwood City Pulse (April 2023) and the San Mateo Daily Journal.

About this page (technical details)
  • Rain that fell: the Belmont Creek rain gauge at Old County Road, 2.4 miles north on the same bay plain. It recorded the whole Dec 2022 storm, so today's rain there is compared with its own record of that storm. The Pulgas gauge, 1.8 miles west in the hills, is closer, but its record starts in 2023; in 10 storms since, it caught about the same as Belmont in the heaviest hour and 10–25% more over 3 to 12 hours.
  • Forecast: the RRFS weather model (AQPI / CW3E), averaged over the 2 grid points in the Cordilleras watershed, the same forecast as on the RRFS rain page. It is compared with the Dec 2022 storm adjusted to the watershed: Belmont's record times the ratio of NOAA Atlas 14 at the watershed's center to Atlas 14 at Belmont (1.09–1.12, about 10% more).
  • Heaviest hour, 6 hours, day: the wettest run of whole clock hours, in the last 2 days and the forecast joined together. The headline goes by whichever comes closest to Dec 2022: under 50% "much less", 50–74% "about half", 75–99% "close to", 100% or more "as much or more".
  • "Once in 25 years" and similar are NOAA Atlas 14 rainfall frequencies (a point value, so a watershed average leans a little low).
  • Dec 31, 2022 in detail: Belmont 4.56 in that day, wettest hour 0.88 in (1:05–2:05 PM), 12 hours 4.12 in; Huddart Park (hills) 7.30 in; Atherton 3.24 in. Redwood City's high tide peaked at 7.8 ft NAVD88 at 7:12 AM, 0.8 ft above the tide table, during the first burst. Basis: lookups/CORDILLERAS_BASIS.md.
