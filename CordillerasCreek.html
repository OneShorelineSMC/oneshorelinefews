What happened on Dec 31, 2022

4.56 inat Belmont on Dec 31, about 4.5 in across Redwood City (third-wettest day on record there)
0.88 inwettest hour at Belmont (1:05–2:05 PM): nearly a 25-year storm
4.12 inin 12 hours at Belmont (3:45 AM–3:40 PM): beyond a 100-year storm there
7.8 ftRedwood City high tide at 7:12 AM, 0.8 ft above the tide table, during the first burst
  • Two bursts. About 4–8 AM and 12–3 PM. The second, wetter hour came near low tide, so the flooding wasn't mainly the Bay.
  • Wet ground first. Belmont had 2.3 in from Dec 26 to 30, and Huddart Park 5.3 in.
  • Where it flooded. The alphabet streets (G Street) and the Centennial and Edgewood Park neighborhoods along the creek in Redwood City. Debris in the channel has been raised since; the creek is privately owned along most of its length.
  • Nearby gauges that day: Huddart Park (hills, 3.2 mi south) 7.30 in; Atherton (Caltrain) 3.24 in.

Sources: OneRain gauge records (Belmont 41104, Huddart Park, Atherton 41106), NOAA tide gauge 9414523, NOAA Atlas 14; Redwood City Pulse and San Mateo Daily Journal on the flooding.

How this is worked out
  • Measured rain: the Belmont Creek rain gauge at Old County Road, 2.4 miles north on the same bay plain. It recorded the whole Dec 2022 storm, so measured rain is compared with Belmont's own record of it. Pulgas, 1.8 miles west in the hills, is closer, but its record starts in 2023; in 10 storms since, it caught about the same as Belmont in the wettest hour and 10–25% more over 3 to 12 hours.
  • Forecast: RRFS (AQPI / CW3E) averaged over the 2 grid points in the Cordilleras watershed, the same forecast as on the RRFS rain page. It is compared with the Dec 2022 storm adjusted to the watershed: Belmont's record times the ratio of NOAA Atlas 14 at the watershed's center to Atlas 14 at Belmont (1.09–1.12, about 10% more).
  • Windows: the wettest 1, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 48 hours, in whole clock hours. "Measured" looks at the last 48 hours; "forecast" at the rest of the forecast run (about 3.5 days); "together" joins the two, for a storm that is underway.
  • Storm size is NOAA Atlas 14 at the watershed's center (a point value, so a watershed average leans a little low).
  • What it can't say: whether the creek will flood. That also depends on debris and blockages in the channel, culverts, how wet the ground already is, and the tide at the outlet (see Tides, Redwood City).
