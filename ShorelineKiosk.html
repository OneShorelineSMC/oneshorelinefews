About this screen

Bay Tides: the NOAA tide gauge in Redwood City. The next 7 days show each day's highest predicted tide (for about the first 2 days, NOAA's model forecast, which adds wind, air pressure and river flow); red marks tides above Mean Higher High Water (MHHW), the average height of the highest tide each day. Tap "Now" for how measured tides compared with predictions over the past week. The bottom row shows NWS coastal flood alerts or forecast notes about tides. Around San Mateo County's King Tide windows, a King Tides line shows the dates (astronomical predictions for San Francisco, without weather).

Creeks: water level (stage) at OneShoreline's creek gauges with their flood thresholds, nearby rainfall, and the Bay tide, all on one timeline. Tap the creek buttons to switch creeks, and "thresholds & notes" for what each level means.

Running higher than predicted means storms, wind or runoff are pushing water higher than the tide alone. Upcoming high tides will likely run higher too; the dashed orange line shows the forecast tide if that continues.

In an emergency, call 911.

Data: NOAA Tides & Currents, National Weather Service, and the OneShoreline Early Warning System (OneRain). Predicted tides do not include storm surge. Data refreshes automatically.
