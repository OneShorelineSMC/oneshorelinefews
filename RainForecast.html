Where this comes from, and how the grid points were picked
  • The model: RRFS (Rapid Refresh Forecast System), NOAA's 3-km model that replaces the HRRR. AQPI at CW3E (Scripps, UC San Diego) posts it for San Mateo County: rain in each hour out to about 3.5 days (from the run every 6 hours) and in each 15 minutes out to about 18 hours (hourly runs). Units aren't stated in the files; they match inches against the NWS forecast and our gauges.
  • Grid points for each creek: every grid point inside the creek's County watershed (C/CAG boundaries; the creek is matched to its watershed in lookups/forecast_points.csv). The forecast is the plain average of those points. A watershed with fewer than 2 points inside also takes the nearest points to its forecast point; Butano, which shares a watershed with Pescadero, uses the 2 points nearest its own forecast point.
  • Ratings: NOAA Atlas 14 at the creek's main rain gauge: Heavy = 1-year storm, Very heavy = 2-year, Intense = 5-year. Atlas 14 is a point value and the forecast an average, so the ratings lean a little low.
  • Checking it: every run is kept in rrfs_log.csv (60 days) to compare with the gauges after storms. Rules: scripts/rrfs.py.
