Storms

Tap a storm for its graphs. A storm starts and ends with at least 6 dry hours at all of the creek's rain gauges and counts with at least 0.10 in at one of them; the creek's response is read until 12 hours after the rain ends, and the storm is then closed and kept as it is.

How this works
  • Data: the creek gauges' readings the data job downloads from OneRain and USGS every few minutes (creeks.json covers the last 50 hours). The storm tracker keeps its own hourly record of the last 10 days and day totals for the season (scripts/storm_tracker.py, storm_tracker.json on the data branch).
  • How rare: the storm's largest amount over 15 min, 30 min and 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours, against NOAA Atlas 14 at that gauge (lookups/rain_frequency.csv): Heavy = 1-year storm, Very heavy = 2-year, Intense = 5-year, with a rough size in years. 15 and 30 minutes are sampled from the data job's rolling totals while the storm is going.
  • Creek response: highest hourly reading from the storm's start to the end of the response window; hours at or above each flood level are hours whose highest reading reached it. Lag: from the hour with the most rain at the creek's own gauge to the creek's peak. Tidal gauges also move with the tide, so their peak can be the tide.
  • Gaps: a gauge that went quiet during a storm is named; its rain may be missing. Rain gauges under-catch in wind.
  • Earlier seasons: the history from the OneRain archive can be added later with the same rules (THRESHOLDS_BASIS.md §12).
