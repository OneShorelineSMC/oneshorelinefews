Season totals by creek since Oct 1

All storms this water year added up for each creek: total rain, the biggest storm, the highest creek level and the hours spent at each flood level, the highest tide during storms, and the days each status was reached.

Rain: the creek's own gauge(s), from the first day this record covers; the main gauge first (the rain gauge at the creek level gauge, like First Avenue for San Bruno Creek), which a storm's rain, how rare and the biggest storm use. Highest creek level: the highest hourly reading on any day this season (a tidal gauge's can be a high tide); hours at each flood level are added up over the season's storms. Status days: days the creek's status reached that level at least once. Reporting: share of data runs (about every 5 minutes) when the gauge had reported recently.

Storms

Tap a storm for its graphs. A storm starts and ends with at least 6 dry hours at all of the creek's rain gauges and counts with at least 0.10 in at one of them; the creek's response is read until 12 hours after the rain ends, and the storm is then closed and kept as it is.

How this works
  • Data: the creek gauges' readings the data job downloads from OneRain and USGS every few minutes (creeks.json covers the last 50 hours). The storm tracker keeps its own hourly record of the last 10 days and day totals for the season (scripts/storm_tracker.py, storm_tracker.json on the data branch).
  • How rare: the storm's largest amount over 15 min, 30 min and 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours, against NOAA Atlas 14 at that gauge (lookups/rain_frequency.csv): Heavy = 1-year storm, Very heavy = 2-year, Intense = 5-year, with a rough size in years and its AEP (annual exceedance probability, the chance of that much rain in that window in any one year): AEP = 1 − e−1/years, since Atlas 14's partial-duration years count every storm, not only each year's largest (1-year ≈ 63%, 2-year ≈ 39%, 5-year ≈ 18%, 10-year ≈ 10%, 100-year ≈ 1%). Below the 1-year storm, the AEP is more than 63%. 15 and 30 minutes are sampled from the data job's rolling totals while the storm is going.
  • Creek response: highest hourly reading from the storm's start to the end of the response window; hours at or above each flood level are hours whose highest reading reached it. Lag: from the hour with the most rain at the creek's own gauge to the creek's peak. Tidal gauges also move with the tide, so their peak can be the tide.
  • Tide: the creek's NOAA tide gauge (Colma, San Bruno, San Mateo and Green Hills: Alameda; Belmont, Atherton and San Francisquito: Redwood City; the coast creeks: Monterey), read hourly: the measured water on each hour and the tide table. "vs the tide table" is the storm push (wind, low pressure, the ocean running high); "vs the average high tide" is against MHHW. Hours at each tide level use the levels on the Tide Tracker page. The record holds 10 days of tide. When this was added (Nov 14, 2026), the first NOAA read went back 5 days, so storms from about Nov 9 on have their tide (marked "added after the storm closed"); earlier storms have none.
  • Road closures: state highway closures in San Mateo County from flooding, slides, washouts, downed trees and storm damage, from Caltrans (scripts/road_closures.py, road_closures.json on the data branch, read every 10 minutes): the Lane Closure System for District 4 (the Emergency Road Closures layer was used too until Caltrans removed it, Nov 2026). A closure counts once Caltrans logs it as started and ends when Caltrans logs it as lifted, or when it leaves the feed (then at the last time it was seen, within 10 minutes). A storm lists the closures that were open at any time from its start to 12 hours after its rain ended, sorted by where: in that creek's watershed, elsewhere on the same side (Bayside or Coastside), and the other side (folded away). Where comes from the closure's points (Caltrans gives coordinates) and San Mateo County's watershed map (data/watersheds.geojson); a closure on a road that crosses the hills, like Hwy 92 from Skyline to Half Moon Bay, can count on both sides. State highways only (101, 280, 380, 1, 35, 82, 84, 92 and their ramps); county and city roads aren't in these feeds. Repairs are often planned days after a storm, so they're marked apart. Started Nov 14, 2026.
  • City posts: cities' own posts about street closures from flooding or storm damage, read from their website feeds every 10 minutes (lookups/city_feeds.csv; San Bruno's News Flash and Alert Center so far). A post shows at the time it was posted, with a link, and counts for the city's creeks and side; posts rarely say when a street reopened. South San Francisco's site has no feed yet, and SMC Alert has no public feed.
  • Gaps: a gauge that went quiet during a storm is named; its rain may be missing. Rain gauges under-catch in wind.
  • Earlier seasons: the history from the OneRain archive can be added later with the same rules (THRESHOLDS_BASIS.md §12).
