    Bay model: datum check at the NOAA gauges

    The model’s water level is relative to its own datum (about mean sea level). At each gauge, the offset to NAVD88 is the median difference between NOAA’s projection at the gauge (ft NAVD88) and the model at the gauge’s nearest model point. “Left over” is the spread after the offset. Each outlet and low spot uses the gauges’ offsets weighted by distance. The model point is the nearest point at least 1.6 ft deep whose level moves with the tide. Model: SFBOFS, four runs a day (about 1 AM, 7 AM, 1 PM and 7 PM Pacific Standard Time), usually available about 2 hours later. Not included: creek flow, waves and wind at the shoreline. scripts/outlet_tides.py · the log (CSV)

    About this map

    Low spots: BCDC (San Francisco Bay Conservation and Development Commission), Adapting to Rising Tides maps, 2017, shoreline overtopping (Bay Shoreline Flood Explorer). Marsh, mudflats and ponds: SFEI Baylands Habitat Map 2020. Buildings and streets: San Mateo County GIS. Critical facilities and transportation: BCDC Regional Shoreline Adaptation Plan (RSAP) data. Flood zones (when switched on): FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer: the 100-year zone has a 1% chance of flooding in any year, the 500-year zone a 0.2% chance. Tides: NOAA tide gauge 9414523, Redwood City. Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors; satellite: Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics.
